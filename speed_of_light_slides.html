A quick question about the universe

How fast is
LIGHT?

Does anyone here know the actual number?

299,792,458
METERS PER SECOND

Approximately 300,000 km/s — the universal speed limit described by Einstein’s theory of relativity.

01 / The limit

Why can’t we reach it?

The closer an object with mass gets to light speed, the more energy it takes to accelerate it further.

The energy wall

Reaching 100% of light speed would require infinite energy.

Objects with mass can get close — but they can never reach the speed of light.

Conceptual curve — the energy rises without limit as speed approaches c.

CERN protons reach about 99.9999991% of light speed. That is a particle-beam record, not a spacecraft record.

02 / Relativity

Time doesn’t pass at the same rate.

Special relativity predicts time dilation: a moving clock runs slower relative to an observer at rest.

ON EARTH
7.09 days

Pass for people waiting at home.

ON THE SHIP
1 day

Pass for the traveler moving at 99% of light speed.

At 90% of c, one day for the traveler is about 2.29 days on Earth. At 99% of c, it is about 7.09 days.

03 / Cosmic distances

Voyager 1: one light-day away.

A light-day is a distance: how far light travels in 24 hours.

One light-day

25.9B km

Approximately 25.9 billion kilometers.

A remarkable journey

Voyager 1 took nearly 49 years to reach this distance.

Its radio signal needs about 24 hours to reach Earth.

VOYAGER 1

Even this distance is tiny compared with the distances between stars.

04 / Looking into the past

When we look at stars, we look back in time.

Light needs time to cross space. We see distant stars as they were when their light began its journey — not necessarily as they are right now.

This is a visual metaphor, not a claim that a particular percentage of visible stars has already died.

05 / The final limit

The closer you get, the harder it gets.

Real physics

As speed approaches c, energy and momentum increase without limit.

Time dilation becomes extreme. An object with mass cannot reach light speed.

Fiction vs. physics

The Flash

In DC Comics, he can break these limits and travel through time. That is a superpower — not a scientific prediction.
